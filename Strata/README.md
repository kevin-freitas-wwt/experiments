# Strata

A voxel landscape generated as you walk through it, a solar system above it, and other suns beyond. One seed word decides where you wake up, how the continents lie, how many planets, asteroids, ships and stations the home system has, and the layout of every system you fly to after it. Everything is rendered in more detail the closer you get.

Live: https://kevin-freitas-wwt.github.io/experiments/Strata/

## Running

ES modules and workers need `http://`, not `file://`:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8765      # from the experiments folder
```

Open http://localhost:8765/Strata/. Everything Strata needs lives in its own folder: Three.js 0.187.0 and the Shippori Mincho font are in `Strata/vendor/` (see the README there for licenses). There is no build step and no network request at all; the folder can be copied anywhere and served as is.

URL parameters: `?seed=word` picks a world, `?at=x,y,z,yaw,pitch,fly` is a place in it (the address bar is rewritten every second as you move, so the current URL is always a shareable link to where you stand; `in=` adds the vessel when you are aboard one), `?r=11` sets the detail radius in chunks (4 to 14; it also scales the heightfield ranges and the distances at which planets and asteroids step up in detail), `?crumb=8` sets breadcrumb spacing in metres, and `?pose=x,y,z,yaw,pitch` frames a still with the overlay hidden (used for the thumbnail).

## How it is made

The world is split into 16×16 columns, 384 blocks tall. Each chunk is built in a Web Worker (a pool of up to four) from nothing but its coordinates and the seed:

1. **Column heights.** A low-frequency value-noise FBM (fractal Brownian motion) decides continent vs. sea; a ridged multifractal adds mountains only where the continent term is strong; a short-wavelength FBM adds roll. The spawn point is drawn from the seed (within 12 km of the origin), and the continent field is offset so that point always lands a few blocks above sea level near a coast. The home world is a disc: beyond about 100 km from spawn the land sinks into deep sea, the heightfield tiles stop at 170 km, and the sea disc ends at 190 km, so from another system it reads as a small flat world rather than an infinite plane.
2. **Biomes.** Temperature (noise minus altitude) and moisture (noise) pick one of nine terrains: open sea, shore, plains, forest, dry basin, tundra, taiga, highlands, snowfield. Each has its own surface stack and tree density.
3. **Trees.** A per-column hash places broadleaf canopies or conifers. The worker generates a 3-block apron around every chunk so trees straddling a boundary are complete on both sides.
4. **Meshing.** Only faces that touch air or water are emitted, each with a palette colour, a face shade, a per-block jitter and baked vertex ambient occlusion (the classic 0-to-3 corner count). Water is a single sheet of top faces, drawn translucent in a second pass with an animated ripple shader (scrolling waves bend the normal and add sparkle in world xz, so it reads the same at home and on any planet shell). Positions, colours, normals and indices come back as transferable buffers.

The main thread keeps a ring of chunks around the player, generating the ones in front of you first (priority is distance weighted by the angle from your heading, re-ranked when you turn; heightfield tiles use the same rule), installs at most three new chunks per frame, and drops anything more than two chunks outside the ring. Above 700 m the ring stops following you; the ground beyond it is carried by three levels of heightfield tiles from the same column function (256 m tiles at 8 m steps out to 3 km, 1 km tiles at 32 m steps out to 12 km, 4 km tiles at 128 m steps out to 40 km), each tile hidden once the finer level fully covers it, with a plain sea disc under everything. There is no fog. Instead a vertex shader drops every ground vertex by d²/2R (R = 90 km), so a horizon appears as you climb and the flat world reads as a small planet from orbit.

## Space

Past 1.5 km the sky thins to black and stars fade in. The home system has up to seven planets drawn from four kinds (lush, icy, rocky, ember; no gas giants), with radii on a rough power law from 1.3 km to 100 km (home-world scale; about one in eight is over 40 km) and orbits spaced so each clears its neighbours and their rings, starting beyond the home disc and reaching 600 km out (temperature, and so kind, is taken from the distance to the home sun), each an icosphere displaced and coloured by 3D noise in a worker at four detail levels (320 to 327,680 triangles) chosen by distance over radius, and within 1.6 radii a 97×97 high-resolution patch of the same surface function is regenerated under you as you move. Within 220 m of the ground the planet becomes voxels: a tangent-plane shell (the same chunk pipeline as the home world, fed by the planet's surface function through a gnomonic projection, with column heights following the true sphere) is attached to the planet with its own frame, so gravity points at the planet's centre and you can land, walk and jump. The shell re-centres when you have moved about 7% of the planet's radius from its origin (64 m on the smallest worlds, up to 400 m on the largest); the old shell stays up until the new one has loaded and then dissolves under it, so nothing blinks. It dissolves the same way when you climb above 350 m. Lush worlds get grass, forest and seas (the voxel shell carves the seabed from the same height field, capped 14 blocks deep, and fills to sea level with water, ice or lava); icy ones snow over ice with frozen seas and the odd conifer; ember worlds basalt and rust around lava seas; rocky ones stone and gravel highlands and dry basins. Lush and icy worlds have atmospheres: the sky dome takes their colours (blue, or pale steel for ice) oriented to the planet's up, thinning to stars with altitude exactly as at home, and hemisphere light follows. Rocky and ember worlds are airless, so their sky stays black at any height and they grow nothing: no trees, no grass, no tundra. The smooth planet mesh is dithered away under the shell so the two never fight; the hole is sized each frame to the radius the voxel chunks actually cover and have finished fading in, so the lower level of detail stays underneath until the voxels are solid and you never see through the surface, and it shrinks again when you leave, with the voxels fading, rather than switching. The smooth sphere, the high-res patch and the voxel shell share one surface function (`planet.js`) for height, biome and colour, the 3D noise is normalised so extra octaves only add detail, and the smooth colours are dimmed to match voxel ambient occlusion, so the three agree in topography and tone to within a block. Columns are 384 blocks tall and each planet shell picks its own vertical origin by sampling the surface across the ring it will cover (plus the sea level), so the lowest valley sits 30 blocks above the bottom and the highest peak fits beneath the top; nothing is flattened into a plateau unless local relief exceeds 370 m, which the terrain amplitudes do not reach. Detail changes everywhere cross-fade with hashed alpha rather than popping, and the seams between voxel ring, fine tiles and coarse tiles are dithered over a band rather than cut. Any planet can carry rings (the first home planet always does, outer icy worlds with 55% odds, inner ones 15%): one to four lanes, each with its own inner and outer radius, thickness, density, opacity, banding frequency and tint, separated by seeded gaps. From a distance a lane is a shader disc with bands and gaps; within about 7 km the disc fades out and the ring resolves into particles generated per 2 km cell of the ring plane (hundreds per cell, sizes on a power law up to 45 m, scattered through the lane's thickness), low-poly instances at range and voxel rocks within 900 m, with their own gravity like any asteroid. Thirty to 220 free asteroids, most of them in a belt around the ringed or biggest body, are low-poly instances until you come within a few kilometres, when each is generated as a voxel blob and meshed with the same ambient-occlusion mesher as the ground. ## The cosmos

Space is divided into 900 km cells and every cell holds one star system at a seeded, jittered position: a star (white, yellow, orange or blue), two to eight planets, some ringed, its own belt, four to twenty ships and up to five stations, all from the cell's hash. Systems within about a thousand kilometres of you exist; the rest do not until you get there. Flight speed keeps scaling with clearance from the nearest body or star, with no practical cap, so a neighbouring system is a few seconds away at full boost, and the same rule slows you on arrival. To keep float precision, the whole scene is re-based around the camera every 50 km of travel; positions you see in the HUD are relative to home. The Travel list and the number keys always refer to the nearest system.

Asteroids have their own gravity: within a few radii of a loaded one you fall toward its centre, land on whichever face is below you and walk around it, with the frame re-oriented every step so up is always away from the middle. Thirteen procedural voxel ships in three classes (skiffs, mid-size hulls with wings, and capital ships with a bridge tower and four engines, up to 650 m long) orbit the planets, and three ring stations, each generated from a torus, a hub, spokes and solar panels, orbit the inner three. Every ship and station has an airlock, marked by an orange door and a pale beacon on the hull; fly within 45 m and press `Enter` to board a procedurally generated interior (ships: a spine corridor with cabins, an engine room and a glazed bridge; stations: a hub with a reactor, spokes and a ring corridor with rooms and outward windows), all voxels at 1 m with glass, lights and panels. `Enter` at the door takes you back out. Interiors ride along inside the moving vessel.

Flight speed scales with clearance: 14 m/s on the deck, growing by that much again for every 120 m of height or distance to the nearest planet or star, and a second factor that doubles it per 30 km of clearance. Holding any thrust key in flight builds a boost from 1× to 8× over four seconds (shown in the HUD; it fades within 6 km of anything and drops the moment you let go), `Shift` triples the lot, and the whole thing caps at 500 km/s. Braking is four times stiffer than acceleration, so arriving at a planet from a cross-system run does not overshoot. Space is under a minute away and the same rule slows you down again on approach, so you can land on a planet without overshooting; planets and stars are solid, nothing else is. Movement on the ground is a swept AABB (axis-aligned bounding box) against the block grid: `E S D F` to walk, `Shift` to sprint, `A` to jump or rise, `Z` to descend, `V` to toggle flight. `B` turns on breadcrumbs, bright red dots dropped every few metres (spacing from the start card or `?crumb=`) so a route can be retraced; `X` clears them. `1` to `7` jump to a planet of the nearest system, `8` to the nearest asteroid, `9` to the nearest ship, `-` to the nearest station and `0` home; `Enter` uses an airlock; a left click with the aiming ring on any planet, star, asteroid, ship or station takes you toward it, halving your clearance to its surface with each click until you are set down on it (ships and stations stop at airlock range, stars at a safe standoff); `P` saves a PNG of the view with no overlays (also a button on the start card, next to copy link); the same list sits under Travel on the start card.

## Palette

Woodblock-print tones: Prussian-blue water, cream mist on the horizon, ochre sand, muted mosses, a vermilion seed stamp. The HUD (heads-up display) is a serif survey card; the seal in the corner is the numeric seed.

## Files

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `src/noise.js` | hash, value noise, FBM, ridged noise |
| `src/blocks.js` | block ids, palette, biome names, constants |
| `src/worker.js` | terrain generation and meshing, heightfield tiles, planet spheres, asteroid and ship voxels |
| `src/pool.js` | priority worker pool shared by everything below |
| `src/world.js` | chunk ring, block queries (home world or a planet shell) |
| `src/shell.js` | ground frames: the home plane, tangent-plane planet shells, vessel interiors, and radial-gravity asteroid frames |
| `src/far.js` | heightfield tile levels and the sea disc |
| `src/planet.js` | planet surface function shared by worker (meshes) and main thread (collision) |
| `src/space.js` | star systems: planets, LOD and near-surface patches, asteroids, ships, stations, stars |
| `src/rings.js` | procedural ring systems: lane shader disc and cell-generated particle field |
| `src/materials.js` | shared materials, horizon-curvature and edge-dither shader patch, hashed-alpha fade-ins |
| `src/player.js` | input, physics, camera (frame changes eased so a new 'up' rolls in over half a second), moving between frames |
| `src/flare.js` | screen-space lens flares for stars (ported from Cosmos), occluded by whatever is in the depth buffer via GPU occlusion queries |
| `src/crumbs.js` | breadcrumb trail (one InstancedMesh, unfogged so it reads at distance) |
| `src/main.js` | scene, sky, lights, HUD, URL parameters |
