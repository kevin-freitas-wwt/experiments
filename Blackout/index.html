Sheep Evades Capture for Ninth Day as Millbrook Loses Patience

By Dana Whitlock · Millbrook Ledger · 6 min read

MILLBROOK — A three-year-old ewe named Clementine has now spent nine days at large in this town of 2,400, outlasting two volunteer search parties, a borrowed drone, and the patience of nearly everyone on Orchard Street.

The sheep slipped through a gap in the fence at Halvorsen Farm on the evening of September 12, according to owner Gil Halvorsen, who said he noticed she was missing only when the rest of the flock refused to come in for the night. "They knew something was off," he said. "Sheep are like that. They won't tell you what, but they know."

"She's been seen at the library, the Lutheran church, and twice behind the hardware store. She has not been seen at the farm."

Since then Clementine has been spotted at least fourteen times, most reliably in the early morning, when she grazes the median on Route 9 before traffic picks up. Police Chief Renata Oduya said the department has fielded more than forty calls, several of them from the same caller, and that officers have been instructed not to pursue on foot after Sergeant Tom Briggs pulled a hamstring on Thursday.

A Town Divided

Not everyone wants her caught. A hand-lettered sign reading "LET HER RUN" appeared on the fence outside the middle school on Monday, and a bakery on Main Street has begun selling a lamb-shaped shortbread it calls the Fugitive. Owner Priya Nair said sales have been "frankly excellent" and declined to comment on whether she has been leaving scraps out back.

Halvorsen, for his part, is less amused. He estimates the ewe is worth about three hundred dollars but says the real cost is to his reputation. "I've kept sheep for thirty-one years," he said. "I've never lost one to the town before. I've lost them to coyotes. This is worse."

A town meeting to discuss what officials are calling "the sheep situation" is scheduled for Thursday at 7 p.m. in the fire hall. Attendees are asked to close the doors behind them.
